First public release of voxim: private, on-device push-to-talk dictation for macOS.

**Hold fn, speak, release.** Your words are typed at the cursor in whatever app you're using.

### Highlights

- **On-device speech recognition.** NVIDIA Parakeet TDT v2 via Core ML; text appears about 0.3 s after release, even after two minutes of talking. Apple's on-device speech is used while the model downloads.
- **Type as you speak:** each sentence appears in your app as soon as you pause, with a live preview of the one in progress. Long dictations finish typing ~0.1 s after you release fn.
- **Hands-free:** double-tap fn to keep listening without holding the key, idling in a small indicator between sentences; tap fn to finish. Finishes after 5 minutes of silence by default (configurable).
- **Works in any app:** editors, browsers, chat, terminals. Your clipboard is restored after typing, and voxim never presses Return.
- **English and 中文:** Auto mode detects the language each time you speak (SenseVoice for Chinese and mixed speech, Parakeet for English), with Simplified or Traditional output.
- **Smart formatting:** a space and lowercase start when continuing a sentence; terminal style (no trailing period) in terminal apps.
- **Vocabulary rules:** "when I say → type" corrections for names and jargon.
- **Safe by default:** it won't type into password fields. If you switch apps mid-dictation, typing pauses and resumes when you come back, so text never lands in the wrong app. Esc cancels.
- **Private:** no audio or text is stored or uploaded. The optional usage stats hold counts and timings only.

### Requirements

- macOS 26 (Tahoe) on Apple silicon
- About 450 MB of disk space for the speech model, downloaded on first launch (another ~450 MB if you turn on Chinese)

### Install

1. Open the DMG and drag **voxim** to **Applications**.
2. Launch it and grant **Accessibility** and **Microphone** (the menu-bar checklist guides you).
3. Set **System Settings › Keyboard › "Press 🌐 key to"** to **Do Nothing**.

### Known limitations

- Early release: there's no auto-update yet. Check this page for new versions.
- Mixed Chinese–English sentences are the weakest case: English terms spoken with a Mandarin accent are sometimes misheard. Vocabulary rules can fix recurring ones.
- Apps that don't expose their text to Accessibility get the transcript without context-aware spacing or capitalization.
